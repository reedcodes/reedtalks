## Lacan's Mirror Stage

The image in the mirror becomes self-image

Notes:

When someone holds up a baby the mirror and says "that's you!" and at some point the baby understands and says "that's me!" but it's not _actually_ the baby, it's an image that then becomes self-image.

The Lassar Glass does the same: it reflects a person's self-image, but in a warped way. For example, Marie is insecure about her appearance and her attractiveness to Alan. In one scene, she looks in the mirror as she turns away from Alan and thinks she hears him say "grotesque cow". In another, the mirror shows her her cesarean scar -- which she is self-conscious of -- as a gaping maw, which terrifies her enough that she attacks her children, the cause of the scar.

Alan worries about his ability to be a good father and husband as he branches out to start his own business. When he is becoming violent, Kaylie tells him that he isn't himself, but Alan looks in the mirror and replies "I've met my demons and they are many. I've seen the devil, and he is me."

Notes:

The mirror is not _impossible_ to defeat. It does have a crack in the lower right corner from when Alan was shot and the bullet then hit the glass -- it was vulnerable as Alan's self-image starts to disassociate from the mirror's influence. But it is very very hard to do! Evil is _hard_ to defeat. It can defend and protect itself, such as when young Kaylie and Tim try their damnest to smash it with Alan's golf clubs.
